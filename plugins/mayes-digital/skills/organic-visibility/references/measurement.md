# Measurement

Read for Search Console, profile and analytics data, reporting, audits, finding classification, priorities, and completion evidence.

## Search Console

Google Search Console supports evaluation of organic Google Search performance.

### Relevant capabilities

Depending on available property access:

- Search impressions.
- Search clicks.
- Click-through rate.
- Average position.
- Search queries.
- Page performance.
- Indexing information.
- Sitemap reporting.
- URL inspection.
- Relevant enhancement reports.

### Requirements

- Confirm the correct property.
- Establish the reporting period.
- Compare equivalent periods where practical.
- Identify material changes.
- Distinguish branded and non-branded queries where useful.
- Identify limitations in available data.

### Interpretation

Average position is an aggregated measurement.

It does not establish that every customer sees the website at the same ranking position.

Search Console does not report every individual search query.

Do not treat missing query data as proof that no searches occurred.

## Google Business Profile Performance

Evaluate available Business Profile performance data to understand local customer discovery and interaction.

### Potential measurements

Subject to current platform availability:

- Profile discovery.
- Search terms.
- Website interactions.
- Telephone interactions.
- Direction requests.
- Booking interactions.

### Requirements

- Verify current metric definitions.
- Confirm the reporting period.
- Distinguish interactions from completed transactions.
- Avoid double-counting overlapping measurements.
- Identify unavailable information.

A telephone interaction does not necessarily establish a completed call or a qualified lead.

A direction request does not establish a completed customer visit.

## Analytics and Organic Attribution

Use appropriate analytics to evaluate website engagement and customer actions.

### Potential measurements

- Organic search sessions.
- Relevant landing-page activity.
- Service-page engagement.
- Contact interactions.
- Quote submissions.
- Appointment requests.
- Ecommerce activity where applicable.

### Requirements

- Respect applicable privacy requirements.
- Use consistent event definitions.
- Distinguish engagement from conversion.
- Verify tracking behavior.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
- Avoid duplicate event tracking.

### Attribution limitations

Recognize that:

- Customers may use multiple channels.
- Consent settings may affect measurement.
- Tracking restrictions may affect available data.
- Offline conversions may not be captured.
- Attribution models may differ.

Do not claim that every lead can be attributed with certainty.

## Organic Visibility Measurement Framework

Measure outcomes at several levels.

| Measurement level | Example metrics |
|---|---|
| Discoverability | Impressions and indexed pages |
| Visibility | Relevant query appearances |
| Engagement | Organic clicks and landing-page activity |
| Intent | Service-page and contact interactions |
| Conversion | Verified inquiries and bookings |
| Qualification | Relevant customer opportunities |
| Business outcome | Attributable completed services or sales |

### Requirements

- Establish a baseline.
- Define meaningful measurements.
- Use consistent reporting periods.
- Identify data limitations.
- Distinguish leading indicators from business outcomes.

Do not report impressions as leads.

Do not report leads as completed sales.

Do not report website traffic as revenue.

## Organic Visibility Reporting

Provide reporting proportional to the service agreement and available data.

### Reporting structure

#### Executive summary

Explain the most consequential developments.

#### Visibility

Report relevant available search and local discovery measurements.

#### Website engagement

Report meaningful landing-page and customer interaction measurements.

#### Lead generation

Report verified inquiries and relevant business opportunities.

#### Completed improvements

Identify changes actually implemented.

#### Opportunities

Identify justified next steps.

#### Limitations

Disclose missing data, tracking restrictions, seasonal effects, and attribution limitations.

### Reporting standards

- Avoid vanity metrics without context.
- Avoid unsupported causal claims.
- Avoid arbitrary SEO scores.
- Avoid misleading percentage changes.
- Distinguish observations from interpretations.
- Explain why measurements matter to the business.

## Ongoing Visibility Management

Organic visibility requires appropriate maintenance.

### Recurring evaluation

Depending on the approved service scope:

- Business information accuracy.
- Service information accuracy.
- Website functionality.
- Indexing status.
- Relevant search performance.
- Business Profile accuracy.
- Review activity.
- Project content opportunities.
- Broken links.
- Technical issues.
- Meaningful content improvements.

### Maintenance principle

Do not make unnecessary changes merely to demonstrate monthly activity.

Recommend improvements when they provide meaningful customer or business value.

A stable, accurate website may require monitoring rather than continual redesign.

## Organic Visibility Audit

Use the following checklist when evaluating an existing website.

### Business foundation

- [ ] Business identity is accurate.
- [ ] Services are accurately represented.
- [ ] Geographic information is accurate.
- [ ] Contact information is consistent.
- [ ] Business hours are accurate.

### Information architecture

- [ ] Important pages have defined purposes.
- [ ] Services are appropriately represented.
- [ ] Navigation supports discovery.
- [ ] Internal links are meaningful.
- [ ] Unnecessary duplicate pages are avoided.

### Content

- [ ] Content addresses customer needs.
- [ ] Service descriptions are useful.
- [ ] Claims are supportable.
- [ ] Geographic references are accurate.
- [ ] Authentic credibility is represented.

### Local visibility

- [ ] Business Profile eligibility is evaluated.
- [ ] Business categories are appropriate.
- [ ] Business information is accurate.
- [ ] Review practices are appropriate.
- [ ] Relevant directory information is evaluated.

### Metadata

- [ ] Important pages have descriptive titles.
- [ ] Meta descriptions are appropriate.
- [ ] Heading structures are meaningful.
- [ ] URLs are understandable.
- [ ] Dynamic metadata is evaluated.

### Technical discoverability

- [ ] Intended pages are accessible.
- [ ] Indexing directives are appropriate.
- [ ] Sitemap behavior is evaluated.
- [ ] Canonical URLs are evaluated.
- [ ] Redirects are evaluated.
- [ ] Structured data is evaluated.

### Customer acquisition

- [ ] Primary conversion actions are identifiable.
- [ ] Contact paths are accessible.
- [ ] Forms are appropriate.
- [ ] Lead destinations are verified where possible.
- [ ] Consent requirements are evaluated.

### Measurement

- [ ] Available reporting sources are identified.
- [ ] Relevant baseline measurements are established.
- [ ] Conversion definitions are understood.
- [ ] Data limitations are disclosed.

## Audit Finding Classification

Classify findings according to evidence.

### Confirmed defect

A demonstrated technical or content problem.

Example:

An important service page contains an unintended noindex directive.

### Confirmed policy issue

A verified departure from an applicable platform policy.

Example:

A Business Profile uses a fabricated physical address.

### Observed opportunity

A relevant improvement supported by available observations.

Example:

An important service has no useful dedicated content.

### Improvement hypothesis

A proposed change that may improve visibility but has not been validated.

Example:

Expanding service-specific explanations may improve relevance for appropriate customer searches.

### Unverified finding

A potential issue that cannot be confirmed with available evidence.

Do not report hypotheses as confirmed ranking problems.

## Visibility Improvement Prioritization

Prioritize improvements according to business relevance and customer impact.

### P0: Critical foundation

Examples:

- Important production pages are unintentionally excluded from indexing.
- Business contact information is incorrect.
- Essential customer inquiry functionality is broken.
- Significant business information is misleading.

### P1: High-value improvement

Examples:

- Important services lack useful content.
- Business Profile information is materially incomplete.
- Significant metadata problems exist.
- Important internal links are broken.

### P2: Sustainable growth opportunity

Examples:

- Additional authentic project content.
- Useful customer question coverage.
- Improved service explanations.
- Relevant local visibility opportunities.

### P3: Optional refinement

Examples:

- Minor metadata wording adjustments.
- Secondary content improvements.
- Nonessential presentation refinements.

Do not prioritize changes solely because an SEO tool assigns them a warning.

Evaluate actual relevance and impact.

## Organic Visibility Recommendation Format

When recommending an improvement, provide:

### Observation

Describe the current condition.

### Evidence

Identify supporting information.

### Customer intent

Explain the relevant customer objective.

### Visibility opportunity

Describe the discoverability or credibility issue.

### Recommendation

Identify the smallest meaningful improvement.

### Implementation

Identify applicable Webflow resources, CMS relationships, metadata, or external platform requirements.

### Measurement

Identify how the outcome can be evaluated.

### Limitations

Disclose missing information, assumptions, and external dependencies.

## Organic Visibility Completion Criteria

An organic visibility task is complete when:

- [ ] The requested scope is understood.
- [ ] Relevant business information is verified.
- [ ] Applicable search intent is evaluated.
- [ ] Recommendations address actual customer needs.
- [ ] Current platform guidance is considered.
- [ ] Core architecture is preserved.
- [ ] Recommendations remain within authorized service scope.
- [ ] Implementation status is accurately reported.
- [ ] Available measurements are accurately reported.
- [ ] Limitations are disclosed.
- [ ] Appropriate next steps are identified.

Completion of an optimization task does not establish improved rankings.

Ranking and business outcomes require subsequent observation and measurement.
